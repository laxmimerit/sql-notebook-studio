# SQL Notebook Studio Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want SQL Notebook Studio to be a welcoming, respectful, and inclusive
community for everyone who uses, contributes to, or maintains the project.

Everyone should be able to participate free from harassment, regardless of age,
disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience,
education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity or orientation.

## Expected behavior

All participants are expected to:

- Treat others with empathy, patience, and respect.
- Welcome newcomers and help people learn without belittling their questions.
- Give constructive feedback about ideas, code, and behavior.
- Discuss technical disagreements in good faith and respect different viewpoints.
- Accept feedback, acknowledge mistakes, and take responsibility for their impact.
- Respect people's boundaries, time, privacy, and accessibility needs.
- Protect confidential information when sharing SQL notebooks, queries, logs,
  screenshots, or examples. Remove credentials, API keys, connection strings
  containing secrets, and personal or proprietary data before posting.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or encouragement of violence.
- Sexualized language or imagery, unwanted sexual attention, or sexual advances.
- Personal attacks, insults, derogatory comments, or deliberate humiliation.
- Repeated disruption, trolling, or continuing unwanted contact after being
  asked to stop.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information
  without their permission.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern, provides evidence, or
  participates in a conduct review.
- Other conduct that reasonably creates a hostile or unsafe environment.

Respectful criticism and disagreement are welcome. They must remain focused on
the work and avoid attacking the people involved.

## Where this applies

This code applies in project spaces, including issues, pull requests, code
reviews, discussions, documentation, and project events or communication channels
when available.

It also applies when someone is officially representing the project in public,
such as through an official project account or as a designated representative
at an event.

## Reporting a concern

**Private reporting email: [uditmerit@gmail.com](mailto:uditmerit@gmail.com)**

Send conduct concerns privately to the address above. Include, where possible:

- A description of what happened and when.
- Links, screenshots, or other relevant evidence.
- The people involved and any immediate concerns.
- A safe way to contact you, if you would like a response.

Do not post sensitive incident details in public issues or pull requests.
If a concern involves the repository owner, GitHub users can also report abusive
accounts or content through
[GitHub's reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
That process is handled by GitHub separately from project moderation.

Reports will be handled discreetly. Information should be shared only with
people who need it to assess or respond to the concern. Absolute confidentiality
cannot be guaranteed.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for applying this code fairly and consistently.
They may remove or edit inappropriate content, limit participation, or take other
reasonable moderation action within project spaces.

When reviewing a report, maintainers should consider the available evidence,
severity, context, impact, and any pattern of previous behavior. They should give
the people involved a reasonable opportunity to share relevant information when
doing so is appropriate and safe.

A maintainer involved in a report should not decide its outcome. Another
uninvolved maintainer should review it where one is available.

## Enforcement

Responses may include:

1. **Correction:** A request to stop the behavior, clarification of expectations,
   and removal of inappropriate content where needed.
2. **Warning:** A written warning describing the violation and the consequences
   of further misconduct.
3. **Temporary restriction:** A time-limited restriction on participation or
   contact in project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction:** Removal from project participation for severe
   misconduct or a sustained pattern of violations.

The response should be proportionate to the behavior. Serious incidents may
justify immediate restrictions without earlier warnings.

Where appropriate, maintainers should communicate the decision, its reason,
and any conditions for returning. A person affected by a decision may request
reconsideration through the private reporting address above,
providing relevant context or new evidence. An uninvolved maintainer should
review that request where one is available.
